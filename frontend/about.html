About the project

Environmental context, made easier to explore.

OzoneGuard AI is an educational project exploring how environmental information can help people understand ozone and UV conditions.

Our approach

From environmental data to useful context.

The project brings several signals into one interface, with clear explanations about what the information can—and cannot—tell us.

What we’re exploring

How a location-focused experience could make ozone, UV and weather information easier to browse and understand.

Why it matters

UV conditions vary over time and from place to place. Clear context can support awareness and informed everyday planning.

How the concept works

A FastAPI backend fetches CAMS composition data and weather forecasts for the chosen point, builds features, and runs two gradient-boosted models: one for ground-level ozone and one for the UV index.

What you can explore

Live conditions for any map point, a 24-hour forecast with measured uncertainty ranges, real history since August 2022, and your own saved locations.

Data and transparency

Nothing is generated in the browser. Ozone and UV come from the CAMS model (Copernicus/ECMWF) via Open-Meteo, weather from Open-Meteo, and total-column ozone from NASA OMI. If a source is down, the page says so instead of showing a number.

Built for learning

The interface uses HTML, CSS and JavaScript with Chart.js, Leaflet and Lucide. The backend is FastAPI with XGBoost models trained on 1.39 million hourly records from 38 places; accounts use Firebase.

OzoneGuard AI is an educational demonstration, not an official monitoring service or a substitute for local public-health guidance. Its ozone and UV values come from an atmospheric model rather than ground sensors, and its forecasts carry measured error, so do not rely on it alone for health or safety decisions.
