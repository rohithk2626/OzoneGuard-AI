About Ozone

Understanding the shield above us.

What is the ozone layer?

A region of the stratosphere (about 15–35 km up) rich in ozone (O₃). Its total amount is measured in Dobson Units (DU).

Why is it important?

It absorbs most of the Sun's harmful UV-B and nearly all UV-C radiation before it reaches the surface.

How does depletion occur?

Chlorine and bromine released from man-made chemicals catalytically destroy ozone molecules, especially over polar regions.

What are CFCs?

Chlorofluorocarbons were used in refrigerants and aerosols. The Montreal Protocol phased them out, and the layer is slowly recovering.

Effect on UV

Less ozone lets more UV-B reach the ground. UV Index also depends on solar angle, clouds, altitude and aerosols.

Why Monitor UV Radiation?

Skin

Sunburn, premature ageing and increased skin-cancer risk.

Eyes

Photokeratitis, cataracts and retinal damage.

Immune system

Overexposure can suppress skin immune responses.

Environment

Harms crops, plankton and aquatic ecosystems; degrades materials.
